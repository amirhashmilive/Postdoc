Executive Summary

Abstract

This doctoral dissertation investigates how CSR communication functions as a strategic driver for societal betterment in central India's primary industrial states — Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand.

Using a mixed-methods design of 400 beneficiary surveys and 40 stakeholder interviews across 10 districts over 5 years (2018–2023), the study analyzes communication channels, linguistic accessibility, corporate transparency, and community perception across education, healthcare, environment, and livelihood sectors.

Findings reveal a striking 26-point language chasm between states, verbal channels outperforming print by 3.4×, and the original Five-Pillar Framework as a normative governance model for future CSR communication reform.

Empirical Results

Key Findings

The 26-Point Language Chasm

Chhattisgarh: 80% local dialect use (Chhattisgarhi/Halbi) vs Jharkhand: 54% (Santhali/Mundari). This 26-point gap directly explains the disparity in CES scores — 2.67 vs 2.14 out of 4.0.

Channel Efficacy Hierarchy

Verbal channels (Gramsabhas, facilitators, workshops) achieved 78% retention vs only 23% for printed pamphlets and billboards in tribal areas.

Sectoral Communication Gaps

Education (38%) and Healthcare (32%) received highest communication investment. Environment (18%) and Livelihood (12%) suffer from acute information deficits.

State Performance Index

Chhattisgarh outperformed Jharkhand on 8 of 9 CSR communication efficacy indicators, attributed to institutionalized Gramsabha consultation mechanisms.
